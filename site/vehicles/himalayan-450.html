The brief

The Himalayan 450 is Royal Enfield's adventure bike: a 452 cc liquid-cooled single making 40.02 PS and 40 Nm, wrapped in a steel twin-spar frame with 43 mm upside-down forks and 200 mm of travel at both ends.

It carries a 17 L tank, a 4-inch round TFT with Google Maps navigation, switchable dual-channel ABS and a 196 kg kerb weight. The standard seat is 825 mm and adjusts to 845 mm; a low seat and a taller rally seat are also offered. It is the most expensive vehicle in this set.

What the numbers say

These are observations from the published figures, not road-test conclusions.

Strengths

  • 40 Nm at 5,500 rpm from 452 cc, with 230 mm clearance and 200 mm suspension travel at both ends.
  • A 17 L tank, 198 kg payload and a 4-inch TFT with Google Maps navigation.
  • A 36-month / 30,000 km transferable warranty and switchable dual-channel ABS.

Trade-offs

  • At 196 kg kerb, it is 27.7 kg heavier than the 390 Duke.
  • The standard seat is 825 mm, rising to 845 mm; the rally seat is 860 mm.
  • It is the most expensive vehicle here, at ₹3.08–3.40 lakh, and makes less peak power than the Duke.
